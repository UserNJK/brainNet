Most "neural networks" are math that borrows the word neuron. BrainNet is the other thing: every cell obeys real biophysics, fires real spikes, and rewires itself through the timing of those spikes, chemical mood signals, and axons that physically grow toward useful partners. No gradient descent. No optimizer. We built it across eight releases, ran deep experiments on learning, awareness, and emergent rhythm — then mapped the whole codebase into a knowledge graph to understand how it fits together.

What is this, really?

Imagine you want to study a brain, but you can't open a skull. So you build one — in software, from the actual rules biologists have measured. You don't train it the way you'd train an image classifier. You set up the physics and the chemistry, press play, and watch what it does on its own: it spikes, it forms habits, it has detectable "thoughts," it can be put under "anaesthesia." That's BrainNet. Here are the seven ideas that unlock the rest of this page.

The one-sentence version: a conventional AI learns by being told its errors and corrected from the top down; BrainNet learns the way tissue does — from the bottom up, through local rules, timing, and chemistry, with no one ever computing a "right answer" to push backward through it.

Case study · the awareness battery

Can you measure consciousness in a simulation?

Read this first — the honest answer is no. The "hard problem" means no external measurement can confirm subjective experience; any dynamical signature could, in principle, occur with nothing being felt. So BrainNet does the scientifically defensible thing instead: it measures the operational markers that consciousness science uses to tell conscious from unconscious states in real brains — and validates them the only honest way, by checking that an awake, integrated network dissociates from unconscious control states. A high score means "this network shows the dynamical signatures that index consciousness in humans and animals." It does not mean the network feels anything.

The design: contrast against three unconscious controls

A marker only counts if the awake network separates from networks that shouldn't be conscious. So the battery runs four versions of the same neurons and compares them.

The five markers, each grounded in a leading theory

Each condition is scored on all five; the scores are z-scored across conditions and averaged into a single graded awareness index. The expected result: AWAKE tops every control.

How to read the output: a clean dissociation — AWAKE above ANESTHETIZED, DISCONNECTED and SHUFFLED on the aggregate index — is evidence of consciousness-like dynamics, not proof of experience. It's a tool for comparing states and architectures, with the epistemics stated up front rather than hidden behind a number.

The headline: the code is the wall, not the card

The RTX 3070 is only ~2.5× faster than CPU at N=3,000 — because the per-step Python loop dominates and leaves the GPU ~99% idle. A fused CUDA / GeNN-style kernel would be ~100–1000× faster on the same hardware. The knowledge graph independently flagged the O(N²) GrowthCone and ThoughtDetector as the next things to sparsify.
